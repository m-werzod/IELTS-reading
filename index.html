The Secret Language of Plants (Short)

For a long time, scientists believed plants were passive organisms. However, research now suggests many plants can communicate by releasing airborne chemical signals. When attacked by insects, some trees emit volatile organic compounds (VOCs) that nearby plants detect.

After receiving these signals, neighboring plants may increase their chemical defenses, making leaves less attractive or even toxic to herbivores. This idea was controversial for decades, but laboratory and field studies have repeatedly supported it.

Plant signaling does not mean plants “think” like humans. Instead, it is a biological response system shaped by evolution. Researchers continue to investigate how far these signals travel and how different species respond.
